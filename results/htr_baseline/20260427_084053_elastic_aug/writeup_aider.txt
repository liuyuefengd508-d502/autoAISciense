For handwritten text, elastic distortions are particularly appealing because they model local deformations that resemble the natural variability of pen strokes and writing style. This idea has a long history in document and character recognition, including the influential recommendations of \citet{simard2003best}. Yet in the extreme tiny-data regime, augmentation is not automatically helpful. If the perturbations are too strong, they may destroy legibility or further destabilize already fragile CTC training; if they are too weak, they may fail to provide meaningful regularization. The practical question, then, is whether carefully tuned elastic distortion still provides a measurable benefit for modern compact HTR models trained with very little data.

Our method augments training images online with mild elastic distortion together with a small random affine transformation, chosen to preserve readability while broadening the visual support seen during optimization. To determine whether any gains are truly caused by augmentation rather than merely by additional training time, we compare the proposed configuration not only with a standard no-augmentation baseline but also with a longer-training control that uses the same optimization budget increase without any input perturbation. This design isolates the contribution of deformation-based regularization from the contribution of extra gradient steps.

\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Handwritten Text Recognition with CTC}
% Introduce the standard line-level HTR formulation and explain why CRNN-CTC models are a natural baseline.
Offline handwritten text recognition maps an input line image to a character sequence without requiring explicit character segmentation. A common approach is to encode the image with a convolutional network, interpret the resulting feature map as a sequence along the horizontal axis, and predict characters with a sequence model trained end-to-end \citep{shi2016crnn}. This formulation is attractive because it converts variable-width line images into variable-length feature sequences while preserving the left-to-right structure of handwriting.

% Explain connectionist temporal classification and its relevance to unsegmented sequence prediction.
Connectionist temporal classification (CTC) provides a training objective for sequence prediction when the alignment between input positions and output symbols is unknown \citep{graves2006ctc}. Given a sequence of frame-wise label distributions, CTC sums over all valid alignments that collapse to the target transcript after removing repeated symbols and blank tokens. This makes it especially suitable for handwritten line recognition, where character boundaries are ambiguous and explicit segmentation is costly or unreliable. In practice, CTC-based systems are simple to train and decode greedily, but they can be sensitive to optimization difficulties when data is limited.

\subsection{Data Augmentation for Document and Handwriting Recognition}
% Introduce augmentation as a source of inductive bias and motivate elastic distortions in particular.
Data augmentation improves generalization by exposing a model to label-preserving perturbations of the training data. In handwriting recognition, useful perturbations should reflect natural variation in stroke shape, slant, spacing, and local deformation while preserving the underlying text identity. \citet{simard2003best} argued that elastic distortions are particularly effective for document analysis because they mimic realistic nonrigid changes in character appearance better than rigid transformations alone. This idea remains relevant for line-level HTR, where small local deformations can plausibly capture writer variability that is underrepresented in a small dataset.

% Clarify why augmentation must be treated carefully in the tiny-data CTC regime.
At the same time, augmentation in a tiny-data setting is delicate. When supervision is scarce, perturbations that are too aggressive may remove information that the recognizer still needs in order to learn stable alignments, especially under CTC training. For this reason, the present work focuses on mild elastic and affine transformations rather than broad augmentation policies. The goal is not to maximize invariance at all costs, but to inject a limited and plausible prior over handwriting variability without making the optimization problem substantially harder.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
% Formally define the supervised learning problem, notation, and evaluation setting used in the paper.
We consider supervised line-level handwritten text recognition on a small dataset of image-transcript pairs
\[
\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},
\]
where each \(x_i\) is a grayscale image of a handwritten text line and each \(y_i = (y_{i,1}, \dots, y_{i,L_i})\) is a target character sequence over a finite alphabet \(\Sigma\). The recognizer produces a distribution over the extended alphabet \(\Sigma' = \Sigma \cup \{\texttt{blank}\}\) at each horizontal time step after convolutional encoding. Let \(f_\theta(x_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{T_i \times |\Sigma'|}\) denote the unnormalized per-step logits for input \(x_i\), where \(T_i\) is the resulting sequence length and \(\theta\) are the model parameters.

% Define the training objective and decoding convention in compact mathematical form.
Training minimizes the empirical CTC objective
\[
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \ell_{\mathrm{CTC}}(f_\theta(x_i), y_i),
\]
where \(\ell_{\mathrm{CTC}}\) is the negative log-likelihood of the target transcript under all valid CTC alignments \citep{graves2006ctc}. At inference time, we use greedy decoding: the most likely label is selected at each time step, repeated labels are merged, and blank symbols are removed to produce the final transcript. This decoding rule is simple and consistent with the lightweight baseline setting studied in this paper.

% State the assumptions that distinguish this work from standard HTR settings.
The defining assumption in our setting is that \(N\) is very small relative to typical HTR benchmarks. Rather than studying performance scaling with large training corpora, we ask how much can be gained from a carefully chosen augmentation prior when only a tiny set of labelled line images is available. This emphasis on extreme data scarcity motivates the use of compact models, conservative perturbations, and character-level evaluation metrics that remain informative even when exact word-level matches are rare.

Given a sequence of frame-wise label distributions, CTC sums over all valid alignments that collapse to the target transcript after removing repeated symbols and blank tokens. This makes it especially suitable for handwritten line recognition, where character boundaries are ambiguous and explicit segmentation is costly or unreliable. In practice, CTC-based systems are simple to train and decode greedily, but they can be sensitive to optimization difficulties when data is limited.

\subsection{Data Augmentation for Document and Handwriting Recognition}

Data augmentation improves generalization by exposing a model to label-preserving perturbations of the training data. At the same time, augmentation in a tiny-data setting is delicate. The goal is not to maximize invariance at all costs, but to inject a limited and plausible prior over handwriting variability without making the optimization problem substantially harder.

% Describe the recognizer architecture used throughout the study and explain why a compact model is appropriate here.
Our method uses a compact CRNN-style baseline that maps a normalized line image \(x\) to per-time-step logits \(f_\theta(x)\) over the extended alphabet \(\Sigma'\). Following the general design of \citet{shi2016crnn}, the model first applies a small convolutional encoder and then interprets the resulting feature map as a left-to-right sequence for recurrent processing. In the implementation used here, the encoder contains three convolutional blocks with ReLU activations, one \(2 \times 2\) max-pooling layer, two \((2,1)\) max-pooling layers that reduce height while preserving width, and a final adaptive average-pooling layer that collapses the feature map to height 1. Given logits \(f_\theta(x_i)\), training minimizes the empirical CTC objective introduced in the background section. We optimize this loss with AdamW \citep{loshchilov2017adamw}, use gradient clipping with maximum norm 5.0, and apply a cosine annealing learning-rate schedule over the prescribed number of epochs. These choices are conventional but useful in a fragile low-resource setting, where optimization can become unstable even for modest models. We also initialize the classifier bias for the CTC blank class to a negative value. This small implementation detail is important because it reduces the model's initial preference for blank emissions and helps it avoid the all-blank local minimum that often appears in tiny-data CTC training.

% Introduce the augmentation policy as the central intervention and define it in the notation of the problem setting.

\[
